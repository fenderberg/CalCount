# Adversarial Review — CalCount PRD v1.1

> Historische reviewmomentopname. Meerdere hieronder genoemde gaten zijn na deze review
> opgelost; raadpleeg de canonieke documenten in `docs/` voor huidige beslissingen.

Scope: findings focus on the new v1.1 material (Epic 3 reactivation, Epic 5, Epic 6, FR14–FR18, NFR9, and the touched Goals/UI/Out-of-Scope/Success Metrics/Decisions/status sections). A few findings on pre-existing material are included where the flaw is glaring.

- **FR14's streak definition is trivially gameable and works against the product's actual goal.** A streak only requires "at least one logged item" per day — logging a single sip of water or a 1-kcal item preserves the streak regardless of whether the user is anywhere near their budget or even engaging meaningfully with tracking. The PRD's own success metric for "Volhouden" (§8) already measures logging days, so Epic 5 risks optimizing a metric that can be satisfied by junk logs, actively undermining the calorie-discipline goal the whole app exists for.

- **FR14 punts its own open question with a parenthetical "to be confirmed at architecture/UX" note, but no owner or decision gate is defined for closing it.** Marking an assumption inline is not the same as tracking it; there's no mechanism in §11's "recommended order" that guarantees this actually gets resolved before Story 5.1 is built, distinct from just being silently carried into implementation as-is.

- **FR15 explicitly declines to define the badge/milestone set** ("de exacte mijlpaal-set wordt bepaald tijdens architectuur/epics-verfijning — geen uitputtende lijst in dit PRD"). That leaves Story 5.2's three acceptance criteria untestable: AC1 ("bereikt vooraf gedefinieerde mijlpalen") cannot be verified against a PRD that provides no actual list, only illustrative examples ("bv."). A PRD that ships an epic with acceptance criteria referencing an undefined artifact is deferring, not specifying.

- **Story 5.1 AC3's streak-reset behavior ("informatief teruggezet — geen bestraffende of beschamende toon") is unfalsifiable as written.** There is no copy example, no defined trigger threshold (what exactly counts as "een gemist dagdeel" — is that a calendar day, a rolling 24h window, the app's day boundary used for budget resets?), and no timezone/day-boundary rule at all, despite the app repeatedly reasoning about "vandaag," "gisteren," and day navigation (FR12) elsewhere.

- **FR16's cadence ("bv. wekelijks") is flagged as an unconfirmed assumption, yet Story 6.1's three ACs are written as if the cadence were settled.** There's no NFR analogous to NFR3 (photo latency) or NFR7 (photo cost target) constraining insight-generation latency, compute cost, or trigger mechanism (cron job vs. on-demand-on-screen-open vs. push notification) — a real gap given Epic 6 is called out in the same breath as introducing new AI cost surface (NFR9) but only NFR9 covers the coach, not the periodic insights.

- **NFR9's cost control for the AI coach is hand-waved relative to the rigor applied elsewhere.** NFR7 gives photo estimation a concrete target ("richtwaarde onder enkele centen per foto"). NFR9 offers only "houdt de kosten per gesprek beheersbaar (bv. gelimiteerde contextlengte)" — no number, no per-day/per-month cap, and critically no protection against volume abuse (nothing stops the single user from asking the coach 200 questions in a day; "limited context length" bounds cost per message, not frequency).

- **Epic 6's data exposure to the AI provider is materially larger than what NFR5 was written to cover, and NFR5 was not updated for it.** NFR5 says health data isn't shared with third parties "buiten de gebruikte AI-provider voor herkenning" (i.e., photo recognition). Story 6.2 AC2 now sends the user's full eating log, budget history, and weight trend to (implicitly) the same or another AI provider as coach context — a broader and more sensitive data flow than a single food photo — yet no NFR addresses retention, logging, or deletion of AI-coach conversation history containing this data.

- **The health/safety framing for AI advice is thinner than the risk warrants.** FR18/NFR8 reduce the safeguard to "gepresenteerd als suggestie/observatie, nooit als medisch advies." This is a calorie-restriction coaching feature built on a single user's real intake/weight data, sitting directly adjacent to disordered-eating risk territory (the PRD already sets a hard safety floor in Decision #9 — 1200/1500 kcal). Nothing requires the AI insights/coach to be aware of or respect that floor, flag concerning patterns, or avoid reinforcing unsafe restriction — "not medical advice" is a liability disclaimer, not a safety design.

- **The Out-of-Scope reinterpretation of "coaching" looks like retroactive scope justification rather than a genuine clarification.** §7 now states that the original v1.0 out-of-scope line for "coaching" meant only human/social coaching, conveniently exempting the newly-desired Epic 6 AI coach. No v1.0 text, quote, or other evidence is cited to support that this was the original intent; as written it reads as the PRD being reverse-engineered to avoid admitting a scope reversal.

- **Epic 3's reactivation is asserted to be "unchanged" scope, but nothing was actually re-validated.** §10 states the epic requires an `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` "to really test/build" and that accuracy/cost are "still to be validated at Epic 3" — the same open item already flagged back in v1.0 (§9 decision #5). Reactivating a paused epic without re-checking whether the AI-model landscape, pricing, or accuracy assumptions changed in the intervening period is exactly the kind of stale assumption an adversarial pass should catch, not wave through as "ongewijzigd."

- **Internal inconsistency in AI model naming between sections.** §4 (Additional Technical Assumptions) proposes `claude-sonnet-5` or `claude-haiku-4-5` for photo recognition, while §9 Decision #5 states the default model is `claude-opus-4-8` with `claude-haiku-4-5` as the cost-saving option. The PRD never reconciles which model is actually the baseline, which is exactly the kind of detail Epic 3's reactivation should have forced a re-check on.

- **The new chat-style "AI-coach" screen structurally conflicts with the PRD's own UX vision.** The Overall UX Vision commits to "één helder getal," minimal friction, and correction via slider/stepper "niet via typen waar mogelijk," explicitly for one-handed mobile operation. An interactive Q&A coach is inherently a typing/reading-heavy surface. The PRD adds this as a bullet-point screen in §3 with zero discussion of how a keyboard-driven chat interface coexists with the "one-handed, no typing" design philosophy it enforces everywhere else.

- **Branding/UX is still explicitly deferred to a separate document, yet Epic 5 and Epic 6 add two entirely new core screens (badges overview, AI coach) with no interaction design beyond one-line ACs.** Given the PRD elsewhere insists on light/non-intrusive treatment for gamification and a careful "not an interruption" framing for AI insights, shipping these epics' acceptance criteria before the UX document exists is optimistic sequencing — §11 itself acknowledges the UX document doesn't exist yet (step 3), meaning the Story 5.x/6.x ACs about tone, prominence, and placement are being asserted ahead of any actual design authority.

- **The "recommended order" in §11 undermines confidence in the ACs already written.** Step 4 says Epics/stories 5 and 6 still need to be "verfijnd tot uitvoerbare taken" and checked for implementation readiness — i.e., the epics as currently written in this very PRD are not yet considered executable — while §9 simultaneously marks the corresponding scope decisions (10–12) as "✅ bevestigd," creating a false impression of settledness for content the PRD's own process says isn't ready.

- **No minimum-data threshold or bootstrapping logic is defined for when periodic insights or badges become meaningful, beyond one illustrative example.** Story 6.1 AC3 mentions "eerste week" as an example of insufficient data, but there's no rule for how much log/weight history is actually required, and no consideration of the fact that Epics 1/2/4 are already live in production — does the "first week" clock start now, or does existing historical data count? Silent on a case that will occur immediately upon release.

- **FR15's example milestone "eerste voortgang richting streefgewicht" conflicts with Epic 4's own design philosophy of trend-over-noise.** Epic 4 tracks and displays weight as a trend specifically because day-to-day weight is noisy (water retention, etc.), yet a badge for "first progress toward target weight" invites awarding (and potentially having to retract) a badge based on a single noisy data point, with no smoothing rule specified.

- **The streak feature has no accommodation for legitimate non-punitive lapses** (illness, travel, deliberate rest from tracking) despite the product being explicitly single-user and low-pressure. Competing implementations commonly use streak-freeze/grace-day mechanisms; this PRD doesn't even raise the question, despite Story 5.1 AC3 going out of its way to promise a "non-punishing" experience — an unaddressed gap between stated intent and actual mechanism.

- **The v1.1 success metrics for gamification and AI-advice are anecdotal, not statistically meaningful, and lack any instrumentation requirement.** "Langste streak groeit over tijd," "ten minste één badge behaald binnen de eerste 2 weken," and "AI-coach ten minste 1× per week geraadpleegd" are single-user, single-sample observations dressed up as success metrics; for a single user who is also the product owner, nearly any usage trivially satisfies them. Nothing in the PRD's Technical Assumptions mentions analytics/telemetry infrastructure to even measure "periodieke inzichten worden gelezen (geopend), niet genegeerd" or the counter-metric's "mediane logtijd" — both are unmeasurable as stated without instrumentation that doesn't exist anywhere in scope.

- **The "tegenmetriek" (counter-metric) in §8 has no baseline to compare against.** It states that if median log time increases "door badges/streak-UI," gamification has become too intrusive — but no current median log time baseline is captured anywhere in the PRD (the existing success metric is a flat "<20 seconds" target, not a tracked baseline), so there is no way to detect the regression the counter-metric is meant to catch.

- **The progress screen is becoming the "overloaded dashboard" the UX vision explicitly disavows.** §3's Overall UX Vision states "geen overladen dashboards," yet the Voortgang & gewicht screen has now accumulated graph + history + periodic AI insights on top of its pre-existing content, with Epic 5's streak indicator also competing for hoofdscherm space — the stated design philosophy and the accreting feature set are on a collision course that the PRD doesn't acknowledge.
